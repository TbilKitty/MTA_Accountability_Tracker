# Structural barriers to documenting missed bus service

## Core contention
Complaint totals are counts of reports received, not a census of service failures. Students, particularly minors, may lack the time, administrative knowledge, internet or telephone access, or incentive to navigate an agency complaint process. A minor should not have to file a complaint for an operator to identify a scheduled trip that failed to provide service. **The prevalence of these barriers is a research question, not a measured majority.**

## The unobservable bus number problem
A rider can report a route, direction, stop, scheduled arrival time and what occurred. When a vehicle never appears, the rider cannot observe its vehicle/fleet number. If a complaint form requests this number, the project must determine whether the field is required, optional, or simply confusing; preserve dated screenshots, instructions, and test results without submitting false complaints. A required vehicle-number field for no-show complaints would be a more serious accessibility/design defect than an optional field.

## Records may exist even without complaints
An MPIA response containing no complaint for a trip does **not** establish that the trip ran, nor that no other records exist. Seek GTFS schedule versions, dispatch and operator assignment records, vehicle location and trip update histories, cancellation logs, customer-service classifications, complaint form instructions, record retention schedules, and known data gaps. Request relevant existing records; an MPIA request ordinarily does not require the agency to create new records.

## Questions the project will test
1. Can a rider report a missed trip without knowing a vehicle number?
2. Are no-show and pass-by complaints accepted and separately coded?
3. What fraction of independently corroborated incidents have a matching agency complaint, for periods where complaint data are available?
4. What are the reporting barriers for school-age riders, measured through voluntary anonymous surveys rather than assumption?
5. Can agency operations data identify a trip using route, stop, date, and scheduled time?

## Legal and statistical limitations
Do not infer a constitutional or statutory violation solely from an incident. Identify the responsible transit and education entities and specific duties separately. A discrepancy between complaints and independently observed incidents is evidence of a reporting gap only after record coverage, retention, and matching rules have been checked. Preserve raw data, hashes, code versions, corrections and uncertainty. Do not expose a child's precise travel routine or identifiable details.
