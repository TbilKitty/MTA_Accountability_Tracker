What the tracker can and cannot observe

The tracker reads MTA’s public real-time feed about every 30 seconds during the morning (6:00 to 9:30 a.m.) and afternoon (2:00 to 5:30 p.m.) school commutes and compares it with the published schedule. It sees buses, not riders: it cannot tell who was waiting at a stop, whether a student missed a bus, or whether anyone reached school late.

Stop event and missed trip
A stop event is one scheduled trip at one timepoint stop (a stop with a published time). One bus trip passes several timepoints, so it adds several stop events. When MTA cancels a whole trip, each of its stops records a canceled event. The Whole trips canceled column counts trips instead: trips on which every Baltimore City timepoint stop was canceled. Read a stop’s canceled events as “the scheduled bus did not come to this stop,” not as separate missed trips.
Disruption rate (canceled or more than 7 minutes late)
The share of classified stop events that MTA’s feed marked canceled or that reached the stop more than 7 minutes after the scheduled time. Classified events have a usable timing estimate or a feed-flagged cancellation. On time means 2 minutes early to 7 minutes late, MTA’s own on-time window.
Monitoring coverage
The share of scheduled stop events the tracker was watching, meaning the feed was working and the trip fell inside the commute window. Events the tracker was not watching are left out of every rate.
Missing vehicle records
Scheduled trips that never appeared in the real-time feed. That can be a missed trip or a bus whose tracking equipment failed, and the two look the same. Because the tracker cannot tell which, these are shown separately and are not counted in the canceled-or-late share.
Possible drive-pasts
Inferred, not reported by MTA. Between two position reports that bracket a stop (normally about 30 seconds apart), the bus averaged at least 15 mph, faster when the reports are farther apart, and the feed never showed it stopped there. A bus may pass a stop legitimately when nobody is waiting and no one asks to get off, and the tracker cannot see riders, so these are possible drive-pasts only. Visits the tracker could not judge (slow traffic, long gaps between reports) are left out of the count, not counted as stops. These records start on November 2.
Small samples and recurring patterns
A rate based on fewer than 20 classified events is flagged Small sample and shown in gray italics, because a few late buses can swing it. School dates mostly disrupted counts the observed school dates on which half or more of that stop’s classified events were canceled or more than 7 minutes late. A stop is flagged Recurring when it has at least 20 classified events, was observed on at least 5 school dates, and was mostly disrupted on at least half of them. A stop observed on fewer than 5 school dates is flagged Few school dates.
